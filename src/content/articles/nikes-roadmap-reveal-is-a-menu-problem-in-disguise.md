Nike spent Monday showing what it plans to ship across 2027 and 2028, and most of the coverage will be about the shoes. There's a new way of building Air cushioning called Tuned Air, a reworked Free, more of the sensory-focused Mind footwear, and fresh versions of the Vaporfly and Vomero. Tuned Air isn't due until late next year, so for now it's a promise rather than a product.

It's a bit like a studio dropping a roadmap trailer for the next two years of content. It's exciting, and it still doesn't change the fact that the shop has to work properly on the day you open it.

## The bit we keep thinking about

Buried in the announcement is a reorganisation of Nike's training range around three ideas: prime, train and recover. Train then splits into five subcategories covering weight training, compression base layers, everyday training, elevated gym staples and a hybrid group. As a way of thinking about an athlete's week, we quite like it, because it follows what people actually do.

As a way of organising a shop, it makes us nervous. Shoppers rarely arrive thinking about priming. They arrive thinking about trainers, or leggings, or something for the gym, and they search with whatever words they already have. A menu built from the brand's vocabulary asks them to learn a new one before they can find anything, and plenty of them won't bother.

We haven't seen how Nike plans to present any of this on its own site, so treat this as a hunch about what to watch rather than a verdict. The thing we'd be looking at is whether the new names sit on top of the familiar ones or replace them.

## Why it matters if you don't sell trainers

Almost every shop ends up here eventually. A rebrand, a new range or a tidy-up produces category names that make perfect sense inside the company and very little outside it. The internal logic is usually sound, and it still has to survive contact with someone typing a few words into a search box at 11pm.

## What to try this week

- Pull your ten most common site searches and check that each one lands somewhere a shopper would expect, using their words rather than yours.
- Look through your menu and category names for anything that only exists inside your business, and add a plain-English label alongside it.
- If you're planning a restructure, ask a handful of people where they'd look for a specific product before you build anything. It takes an afternoon and saves a lot of redirects later.

We'll come back to this once Nike's new categories are live, because that's when we can look at what the shop actually does and not just what the announcement says.
