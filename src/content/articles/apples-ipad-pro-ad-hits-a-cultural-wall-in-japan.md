In May 2024, Apple’s “Crush!” advert showed a hydraulic press destroying creative tools before revealing an iPad Pro. The image offered an easy reading: many capabilities could fit inside one thin device. It also offered a much less flattering one: technology was crushing the things people loved.

[ITmedia’s contemporary account](https://www.itmedia.co.jp/mobile/articles/2405/20/news071.html) covered the criticism and Apple’s apology. It also noted that negative reactions extended beyond Japan. The response should not be presented as the view of an entire country.

## Our view

The campaign’s weakness was the emotional meaning of its central image. A musical instrument can carry years of practice. To its owner, it may hold memories that a tablet cannot replace.

For a company selling creative tools, destruction was a risky way to show progress. The viewer had to accept the loss before reaching the product reveal. If that first moment felt hostile, the closing message had little chance to repair it.

[Christianity Today’s response](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2024/05/pastor-apple-ipad-ad-apology-crush-reality/) explored the value of physical creative practice and linked to the earlier version of this page. That criticism helps explain the dispute without requiring a claim that every artist reacted alike.

Our reading is that the advert treated the objects as functions that could be compressed. Some viewers treated them as things worthy of care. The gap between those readings is a useful subject for campaign research.

This does not establish a single Japanese attitude towards objects. Cultural claims need more evidence than a selection of social posts.

## What to do about it

Test a campaign concept with people who use the tools or practise the craft it depicts. Ask what they think the advert says about them before explaining your intended message.

For an international campaign, include local reviewers early enough to change the concept. A translation check alone will not reveal how its imagery might land.
