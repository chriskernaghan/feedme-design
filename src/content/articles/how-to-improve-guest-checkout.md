A customer should be able to buy without working out how to avoid registration.

This guide is for shop owners and designers reviewing a retail checkout. By the end, you will have a clear guest route and a short test for finding account prompts that interrupt it.

It builds on our research review, [Let customers finish buying before asking them to join](../account-creation-checkout.md), and [Baymard’s documented checkout examples](https://baymard.com/research-articles/make-guest-checkout-prominent). The evidence comes from Baymard’s testing, not an audit carried out by feedme.design.

## What to change

### Name the guest route

Use a button labelled “Continue as guest”. In [Baymard’s examples](https://baymard.com/research-articles/make-guest-checkout-prominent), indirect wording left users unsure whether they needed an account.

For a checkout that begins with an email field, put “No account needed” beside the introduction. Use that wording only if someone can finish the purchase without registering.

The email address may still be needed for the receipt. Explain that purpose beside the field so the customer can understand why you are asking.

### Put it where someone will see it

Make guest checkout a prominent button near the top of the account-choice screen. Baymard’s [Power.dk example](https://baymard.com/research-articles/make-guest-checkout-prominent) shows participants finding this arrangement with ease; subdued links were easier to miss.

Open your proposed layout on a small phone. Check it with your cookie notice visible, rather than testing only the tidy design file. The practical question is whether the customer can see the guest action when the page first appears.

### Ask about an account after the order

Remove registration prompts from the guest journey and place an optional invitation on the confirmation page. Baymard’s [Puritan’s Pride example](https://baymard.com/research-articles/delayed-account-creation) shows a second invitation making a participant think registration was required.

An example to adapt is: “Your order is confirmed. Create an account to view your order history.” Check that your account system offers that benefit before using the copy.

### Explain when registration will be available

If customers can register after buying, say so at the start. In [Baymard’s Stance and Williams Sonoma examples](https://baymard.com/research-articles/delayed-account-creation), this helped reassure people choosing the guest route.

A short line beneath the button is enough to test: “You can create an account after placing your order.” It should describe what the checkout will do.

## How to check it worked

Allow about five minutes for a first check. Use your shop’s test environment when completing payment.

Start signed out on a phone. Ask someone unfamiliar with the shop to buy one item without registering. Do not point out the guest button. Note where they first tap and any moment when they think an account is compulsory.

Continue to confirmation. The check passes when they complete the order without a password and understand that registration is a separate choice. If they hesitate, record the exact wording on screen before changing it.

Repeat with a test email already linked to an account. Confirm that this route does not trap someone in password recovery when guest purchasing is supported.

This checks whether the journey works and makes sense. It cannot prove a sales uplift. For that, compare checkout completion in a controlled experiment with enough traffic to support a conclusion.
