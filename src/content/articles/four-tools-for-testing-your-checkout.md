Testing a checkout means answering three different questions, and no single tool answers all of them. You want to know what people actually do, where in the form they give up, and whether a change you made helped. Here are four tools that each take one of those jobs.

A quick note on how this list was put together. We haven't used any of these tools ourselves for this piece, so everything below comes from what each vendor says on its own pages as of 17 November 2026, and prices change, so check before you buy. We have no commercial relationship with any of them, and none of the links here earn us anything.

## 1. Microsoft Clarity: see what people do

Clarity is a behaviour analytics tool with heatmaps and session recordings, and Microsoft describes it as completely free. It has a Shopify integration that looks at the checkout funnel, and it works on other sites by adding a script.

**Good for:** watching real sessions to find the moment someone hesitates, taps the wrong thing or leaves. **Worth knowing:** it shows you what happened. It doesn't run experiments, so you'll need something else to test a fix.

## 2. Lucky Orange: recordings with funnels and surveys

Lucky Orange bundles heatmaps, recordings, funnels, surveys and live chat, and its own pages say every feature is on every plan, with the price depending on monthly sessions. A comparison page on its site lists a plan at $39 a month, or $32 a month billed annually, for up to 3,500 sessions, and a 15,000 session plan at $89 a month. Its Shopify listing mentions a free plan.

The vendor also says it can record and map Shopify Plus's hosted checkout pages, which many analytics tools can't see into. That's the vendor's claim and we haven't checked it, but it's worth asking about if you're on Plus.

**Good for:** shops that want recordings plus surveys in one place. **Worth knowing:** pricing is by session volume, so a busy shop will move up the plans.

## 3. Zuko: where in the checkout form people give up

Zuko tracks what happens inside forms, and it has a Shopify app that reports at the level of individual checkout fields, which tells you which field causes hesitation, errors or abandonment. Its Shopify plans are free for 500 checkout sessions a month, then $29 a month for 5,000, $49 for 10,000 and $99 for 25,000. The vendor says it won't add automatic overage charges and will tell you when you outgrow a plan. Form tracking outside Shopify is priced separately.

**Good for:** finding the specific field that's costing you orders, such as an address box that keeps throwing errors. It pairs well with our guide to [clearer mobile checkout fields](/articles/how-to-clarify-mobile-checkout-fields/). **Worth knowing:** it measures where people leave and doesn't watch them, so it works best alongside recordings.

## 4. Convert: prove a change worked

Convert is an A/B testing platform. Its Growth plan is $399 a month, or $299 a month billed annually, for 100,000 tested users a month, and Pro is $599, or $420 billed annually. There's no free plan, but Growth and Pro have a 15-day free trial that doesn't need a credit card.

**Good for:** shops with enough traffic to run proper experiments. **Worth knowing:** an A/B test only gives a reliable answer with enough visitors, so for a smaller shop the tools above will usually tell you more for the money, and a controlled test makes sense once you have a change worth proving.

## Which one first?

If you're not sure where your checkout loses people, start with recordings (Clarity is free, so it costs nothing to look). If you already suspect the form, add Zuko to find the field. Leave A/B testing until you have a specific change to test and enough traffic to judge it, which is how we'd approach the tests described in our guides, such as [how to make guest checkout easy to find](/articles/how-to-improve-guest-checkout/).

## Before you install anything

Recording tools can capture personal details typed into a checkout. Check that the tool masks payment and personal fields, and that your cookie consent covers it before it runs on customers' sessions. Ask the vendor how it handles that, and read their answer carefully.

## What we left out

Hotjar, VWO and a few other well-known names aren't here, because we couldn't confirm their current plans and limits from the vendors' own pages today, and third-party sites gave conflicting numbers. We'd rather leave a tool out than quote a price we can't back up, and we'll add them when we can.
