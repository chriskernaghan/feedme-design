> **Archive note:** This is a newly researched replacement for an unavailable Feed Me Design article. It was prepared on 13 November 2026 and is not a recovered copy of the original.

Canva’s 2024 Teams pricing dispute is worth revisiting because of what followed it. In its [Pricing Promise announcement](https://www.canva.com/newsroom/news/pricing-promise/), Canva said early Teams customers would keep their original pricing for their current team. The company also set out principles for how it would handle future pricing decisions.

That response belongs in any account of the controversy. This is a look back at the dispute, rather than a guide to Canva’s current plans.

## Our view

The argument made on this site at the time still matters. [Fortune quoted Chris Kernaghan](https://fortune.com/2024/09/03/canva-hiking-teams-subscription-prices-ai-features/):

> “Canva’s strategy risks pricing out the smaller teams that benefit most from its platform.”

An expanding feature list can make a higher price seem reasonable inside a company. A customer may see the same change through a much smaller task: making next week’s shop promotion.

If that task has not become easier, the extra features may carry little value for them. A team with a modest budget can like the product and still struggle to justify its renewal.

Our view is that pricing changes need research with existing customers. Ask which work the product helps them complete and what that work is worth. Someone who rarely uses a new capability should still be able to understand why their bill is changing.

Canva’s decision to retain early customers’ pricing shows that the terms of a transition can change. It does not tell us how many people would have left, and we should not invent that figure.

## What to do about it

Before changing a subscription price, show existing customers the total bill they would face after any introductory discount. Ask them to explain the change back to you.

If you buy design software for a small team, review actual use before renewal. Compare the cost with the work it saves, using the price shown in your own account.
