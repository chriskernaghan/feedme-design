The VAIO logo has a documented idea behind its shape. In its [1997 announcement](https://www.sony.com/en/SonyInfo/News/Press/199706/97CI-056/), Sony described the mark as a meeting of analogue and digital worlds. [VAIO’s brand account](https://www.vaio.com.sg/about) explains the wave formed by V and A, followed by I and O as the digital digits one and zero.

That makes the familiar explanation more than a theory passed around design blogs. It came from the brand itself.

## Our view

The appeal is the fit between the idea and the product. A computer brand associated with audio and video had a reason to connect those two worlds. The meaning adds something useful when a viewer discovers it.

The mark also works before that discovery. Its joined letterforms create a distinctive outline that can be recognised as a name. A person does not need to solve the symbolism to identify the product.

That is a useful distinction when reviewing a logo. A clever presentation can make almost any shape sound meaningful. The harder question is whether it still works when the designer stops explaining it.

Minimalism is not a score based on how many lines a designer removed. A mark can be spare and still be hard to read. It can also contain detail without losing its identity. VAIO is worth studying for the relationship between its form and its purpose.

## What to do about it

Review a proposed logo without the concept slides. Try it at the smallest size your customers will see and on a plain background.

Then ask people what name they read. If the basic identification fails, fix that before adding more symbolism. The story behind a logo should reward attention without becoming a condition of understanding it.
